What this guide covers

Before you start

Three pieces of housekeeping sit outside the wizard. None of them takes long, and the first one matters more than it looks.

Verify your email

Signal asks for this on the dashboard checklist because password recovery and notifications both depend on it. If you never verify, you can still use the product, but you cannot recover the account yourself and scan-completion notices have nowhere to go.

Name your workspace

A workspace holds your projects. The default name is literally "Workspace", which is fine for one person and confusing for a team. Rename it in Settings before you invite anyone.

Understand the unit: one scan

Everything in Signal is counted in scans. Starting a scan uses one, and that is true whether the scan succeeds, finishes with degraded results, or fails. A failure is not a free retry.

Your allowance is a rolling 30-day window, not a calendar month. The window resets when your oldest counted scan drops out of it, which is why the start-scan page shows "resets in N days" rather than a date at the start of the month.

Why this matters before step 1

On the free Starter plan the allowance is one scan per 30-day window. If you launch a scan before you have set the market and the language, you have spent that scan measuring a project that is not configured, and you wait out the window before you can measure it properly.

So: finish steps 1 and 2 first. Then scan.

01

The domain to track

Step 1 of 5. Required. This is the site Signal audits against search and the answer engines.

The decision you are making

You are choosing the single domain that this project represents. Every score, every fix and every comparison in the project is tied to it. A project tracks one domain, not a set of them.

The field wants a bare domain, for example yourdomain.com. You can paste a full URL: the scheme, the www. prefix and anything after the first slash are stripped for you, and the result is lowercased. What is not accepted is a brand name on its own, or anything without a dot in it, because that is not an address Signal can fetch.

What we recommend

Use the canonical public domain your customers actually reach, the one that does not redirect anywhere else. Not a staging host, not a marketing subdomain you are about to retire, not a deep landing-page path.

The reason is comparability. Scores are only ever compared with scores for the same target, so the domain you pick here is the identity your history accumulates against. Changing it later does not carry that history across.

If you skip it

You cannot. Continue stays blocked, and the field turns red, until the value looks like a domain. This is the one place in the wizard where there is no Skip.

You know it worked when

Continue advances you to step 2, and the field keeps the cleaned-up version of what you typed rather than the raw paste.

Note that the project itself is not created yet. Signal creates it at the end of step 2, once it also knows your market and language, so there is no half-made project sitting in your workspace if you stop here. If you leave the project name blank, the domain becomes the name.

02

Market and reporting language

Step 2 of 5. Required. Where your audience searches, and which language reports are written in.

The decision you are making

Target market
A country, or Global

Choose from Global plus a full country list. Global is the honest answer when you genuinely do not have one market, and it records no target country rather than a made-up one.

Reporting language
English or Arabic

These are the two languages Signal produces reports in. An Arabic report is typeset right to left throughout, including the PDF.

Why the market field earns more care than it looks

The market is not a label on a dashboard. It travels into the audit itself: it is used when Signal works out who your competitors are, and it is written into the report as the market the site was assessed for.

That is also why the field is a proper list rather than free text. A plausible-looking value that is not a real country code once reached the analysis stage on a site whose homepage was missing its title and heading, and the model read that value as the brand name and recommended putting it in the page title. Picking from the list removes that whole class of mistake.

What we recommend

Pick the one country that most of your buyers are in, even if you also sell elsewhere. A specific market gives sharper competitor discovery than Global does.

Choose Global only if you really have no primary market. Do not pick a country you are hoping to expand into: the audit will assess you as if you already compete there.

For language, pick the language the people who will read the report speak. Both English and Arabic are fully supported, so this is a reader decision, not a capability decision.

If you skip it

You cannot skip the step, but you can leave the defaults. The defaults are Global and English, which is a legitimate configuration and not an error state. It simply means Signal has no country to narrow competitor discovery with.

You know it worked when

Pressing Continue creates the project. The button shows a working state while that happens, then step 3 opens. From that moment the project exists in your workspace and appears on the dashboard, even if you abandon the rest of the wizard.

If creation fails, the values you typed are kept so you can retry without re-entering them. Both fields remain editable later in project Settings.

03

Competitors to track

Step 3 of 5. Optional, up to 5. This is who Signal benchmarks you against.

The decision you are making

You are naming the sites you want to be measured next to. Enter them as domains, one at a time. A brand name is not enough: Signal needs the address, because it has to fetch and measure the site. Duplicates are rejected, and the wizard stops you at five.

Check your plan before you fill this in

Competitor tracking is included from the Specialist plan upwards. On Starter and Standard it is not part of the plan, and the platform refuses to save a competitor with a message naming Specialist. In the wizard that appears as an error and the step does not advance.

On Starter or Standard

Leave this step empty and use Skip / Continue. Trying to add a competitor here will not work, and it is a plan boundary rather than a bug.

What we recommend

If your plan includes it, add three to five real, direct rivals: the sites a buyer would genuinely shortlist against you. Each one as its own domain.

Resist the urge to add the biggest names in your sector if you do not actually compete with them. A comparison against a brand operating at a different scale tells you very little you can act on, and it occupies one of only five slots.

You do not have to decide now. Competitors can be added, renamed and removed later from project Settings, and adding one does not require a new scan to take effect on the next one.

If you skip it

Competitor comparison stays empty until you add one. That is the product's own wording, and it is literal: the comparison surfaces have nothing to show, and the competitor-comparison stage of a scan has nothing to compare you against. Nothing else about your scan changes, and no score is penalised.

You know it worked when

Each competitor appears immediately as a chip with a remove control next to it. After the wizard they show under project Settings, in the Tracked competitors section, and on the project's Competitors page.

04

Connect Google Analytics

Step 4 of 5. Optional, but it is how traffic and channel mix get real numbers.

The decision you are making

You are deciding whether Signal may read your analytics data. Everything else in Signal is measured from the outside: your pages, the search signals, the answer engines. Traffic is the one thing that can only come from you.

The privacy boundary, as the product states it

"Signal reads only the traffic data you authorise. Nothing else in your account is touched, and you can disconnect any time."

How the connection actually works

Connect GA4 starts a real Google sign-in. You leave Signal, approve the access at Google, and come back. Because of that, a click on the button never by itself marks you as connected: when you return, Signal re-reads the connection state from its own records and only then shows Connected. If you abandon the Google screen, nothing is recorded.

If the environment you are using has no Google credentials configured at all, no Connect button is shown and the step says so plainly instead of offering something that cannot work.

The part that is easy to miss

Connecting Google is the first of two moves. The connection is held at workspace level, and the project still needs to be pointed at the specific analytics property that belongs to it. You do that in project Settings, under Connected data: choose the Google Analytics account and switch on Use this source.

Until a property is bound to the project, session figures correctly report that they come from Google Analytics and that no property is connected. The connection is real; the project just has not been told which property to read.

What we recommend

Connect it, and bind the property in the same sitting. The two-step shape is the single most common reason a customer believes analytics is connected while the traffic section stays empty.

If you do not own the analytics account, skip the step now rather than waiting on someone. Signal supports connecting it later, and project Settings also allows access shared by a client as the source instead of your own account.

If you skip it

The traffic section stays empty until you connect. Scores, fixes and answer-engine measurement are unaffected: none of them is derived from analytics.

You know it worked when
  • The wizard button reads Connected and is no longer clickable.
  • In project Settings, under Connected data, the Google Analytics row shows Connected with an account selected.
  • The project's traffic figures stop reporting that no property is connected.
05

Your first scan

Step 5 of 5. The point of all of it: your first audit results.

What Finish actually does

Run my first scan takes you to the start-scan page for the project you just created. It does not launch anything yet. That page is where you see your allowance, choose how thorough the scan should be, and press Start the audit. Nothing is spent until you do.

This is deliberate. The allowance is stated before you commit, not at the moment of refusal: your plan, your limit, how many scans are left, when the window resets, and the line "This scan uses 1 of your remaining scans."

Choosing the depth

Depth trades coverage for time. These are the product's own figures, shown in the same table you will see on the page. They are typical estimates rather than commitments, and a large site takes longer.

What we recommend

Run Fast first. It is the default, it is available on every plan, and in a few minutes you have a baseline and a fix list. Spend the deeper tiers once you have worked through the obvious problems, so the extra detail is about things you have not already found.

If you are on Starter, with one scan per 30-day window, this is not a close call: Fast, and only after steps 1 and 2 are set correctly.

What is measured, and what is not

The answer engines Signal measures against are listed on the start-scan page. Every plan measures all of them; choosing a subset is the only part of that section that depends on your plan.

ChatGPTGeminiPerplexityClaudeGrokDeepSeek

There is no fixed number of prompts in a scan. Prompts are the questions you choose to track for the project, and your plan sets how many can be active at once. The product shows your own cap with how many you have used and how many remain, so you never have to guess it.

A new project starts with no prompts

That means the "Appearance in AI answers" figure has nothing to compute from after your first scan, and says so rather than showing a zero. Add prompts from the AIO detail page once you have your baseline, and that figure starts filling in.

06

While it runs, and what lands on the dashboard

Signal reports status per stage, not a percentage. Each stage shows its honest state word.

The five stages

Crawl
Key pages fetched and rendered
Technical signals
Indexation, structured data, speed
Answer-engine measurement
Testing prompts across the measured engines
Competitor comparison
Against the competitors you track
Synthesis
New scores, deltas and the updated fix list

Each row carries one of a small set of state words: queued, running, succeeded, degraded, failed or skipped. There is no progress bar, because a percentage would be invented. A stage that could not finish says which one it was, and the stages that completed before it are kept.

It is safe to leave the page

The scan runs on Signal's side. Close the tab, come back later, and the page resumes where the scan actually is. The result lands on your dashboard either way.

What you get when it succeeds

AIO score
Out of 100

How clearly your website and business information can support AI answers.

SEO score
Out of 100

How ready your website is for Google, based on content and technical checks.

You know it worked when
  • The run page reads succeeded, and tells you how long the scan took.
  • It confirms one scan was used, so the allowance figure you saw before launching is reconciled.
  • Your AIO score and SEO score are shown. If this is the first scan for this target, Signal says there is nothing to compare yet rather than inventing a change. From the second scan on, it names the date of the previous one and reports what moved, one line per score, and says plainly when no score moved at all.
  • You are offered three next steps: open the AIO detail, open the SEO detail, or review the fix plan.
If it fails

The scan still counted against your allowance. The run page names the stage that could not finish and keeps the stages that completed, so a retry is an informed decision rather than a blind one. Where the failure happened before the job was accepted, the message says explicitly that no scan was used.

07

Your first week

One scan is a baseline, not an outcome. The value is in the loop: read, dismiss, fix, rescan, compare.

1. Read the action plan

Every fix Signal found, ranked by impact per effort. The project dashboard shows the top three; the action plan page shows all of them, with filters by category and status.

The action plan page is included from the Standard plan upwards. On Starter it is shown locked with the plan that unlocks it named. The dashboard's own top-three preview currently unlocks one tier higher, on Specialist, so on Standard you reach the full plan from the project's Fixes page rather than from that card.

Why impact is a word, not a number

Fixes carry a categorical impact rather than a point estimate. Signal publishes a figure only once there is enough completed history to say what a fix actually moved. A fix the audit did not rate says so, instead of showing a default dressed up as a measurement. Treat the ranking as an ordering, not an arithmetic promise.

2. Dismiss what does not apply

Dismissing is stronger than hiding. It tells the Advisor not to recommend the fix again: it is suppressed from future audits, not just removed from this list. Dismissal is keyed to the underlying problem, so the same fix coming back in different words is still recognised as one you dismissed.

What we recommend

Dismiss the handful of items that are genuinely wrong for your business, a deliberate choice rather than an oversight. Do not dismiss things you simply are not ready to do: that is what the status filters are for, and a dismissal is meant to be a lasting instruction.

3. Mark done what you have actually fixed

Marking a fix done records your claim and says so: Signal will confirm it on the next scan. After that scan the item reads one of three honest things: the latest check no longer finds this problem, or the latest check still sees this problem, or no check has confirmed it yet. That third state is why the loop needs a second scan to be worth anything.

4. Rescan, then compare

Use Rescan from the project dashboard. Check your remaining allowance first: on a plan with few scans, rescanning after every single change burns the window without producing a readable difference.

What we recommend

Batch your changes. Work through the fixes you intend to make, then rescan once. The comparison is between two points, so the fewer unrelated changes sit between them, the more clearly you can read which ones mattered.

Then read the comparison rather than the absolute scores. "Moved from 54 to 61" is information. "61" on its own is not.

A working first week looks like this
  • Project created, market and language set deliberately.
  • One baseline scan, succeeded, with both scores recorded.
  • The action plan read once end to end, with the items that do not apply dismissed.
  • A batch of fixes made and marked done.
  • A second scan, and a comparison you can point at.
